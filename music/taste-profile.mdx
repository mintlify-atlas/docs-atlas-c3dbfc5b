Every music-related decision in OpenSwarm traces back to a single file: `knowledge/music_context.md`. This is the authoritative taste profile — a living document that defines genre identity, BPM window, label pedigree, essential reference cuts, sonic palette, and production DNA. It's not a configuration file in the traditional sense. It's a musical identity document that every agent reads before any creative or curatorial task. The Music Curation engine reads it to score discovery candidates. The Ableton Dream Agent reads it before generating a nightly sketch. The Reference Finder reads it to suggest tracks. When you change this file, the change propagates everywhere simultaneously.

## Genre identity

Unkle Funk's sound is rooted in the original Chicago and Detroit house traditions — not as nostalgia, but as a living lineage to push forward.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Deep House" icon="house">
    The original meaning: warm, chord-heavy, spiritual, Larry Heard territory. Not the watered-down "deep" label slapped on anything with a pad. Breathing chords, sub bass that moves air, Rhodes and Wurlitzer.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Soulful House" icon="heart">
    Vocals that feel lived-in, gospel undertones, Kerri Chandler / Dennis Ferrer / Masters At Work lineage. Not pop-soul-house. The vocal supports the groove — it doesn't lead it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Classic Chicago House" icon="music">
    Trax Records, Jackmaster, Ron Hardy, Ron Trent era. Raw drum machines, 303 basslines, spiritual grit. The chord structure of Mr. Fingers' "Can You Feel It" — the entire deep house emotional vocabulary in one riff.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Detroit Deep Soul" icon="star">
    Moodymann, Theo Parrish — jazz-influenced, unpolished by design, emotionally complex. Lo-fi texture as artistic statement. Soul without apology.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### BPM and feel

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **BPM window** | ~118–128 |
| **Sweet spot** | 120–123 (123 is dead center, data-confirmed) |
| **Key preference** | Minor keys — C Minor, A Minor, D Minor |
| **Groove** | Swung, not quantized-rigid. Human. Breathing room between notes. |
| **Arrangement** | Long intros, patient builds, no rush to the drop. Tracks that reward DJs who know how to mix. |

### Anti-patterns

These are disqualifying in any curatorial or creative context:

- EDM aesthetics, side-chain pumping as a featured effect, festival builds and drops
- Vocal chops as the melodic hook
- Anything polished, radio-safe, or commercial
- Big room, future house, industrial tech house noise
- Arpeggiators running obvious major-key patterns
- Mid-range-heavy mixdowns — this music lives in the lows

## Measured taste fingerprint

Beyond the qualitative taste profile, the Dream Agent's instructions embed a data-proven fingerprint derived from analysis of 139 confirmed favorites compared against 655 reference-label tracks. These findings sharpen what the subjective preferences actually mean in measurable terms:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Rejects the loudness war" icon="volume-slash">
    His favorites are the most dynamic, least-compressed tracks in the dataset (MFCC-1 of −156 vs commercial average of −46). His ear values headroom and dynamic range. In production terms: loose humanization, wide velocity variation, never flat-line dynamics.
  </Card>
  <Card title="123 BPM is dead center" icon="metronome">
    Confirmed by corrected analysis — not an approximation. The viable range is 122–124; wandering outside that needs a reason. 120 feels slow and heavy; 126 starts to lean toward New York house energy.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Warm but clear" icon="sun">
    Spectral centroid sits around ~3050 Hz. Avoid harsh or piercing highs; avoid muddy lows. The target is warm, present, and clean — the kind of clarity you get from analog saturation rather than digital sheen.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Harmony always" icon="chord">
    Chroma value of ~0.55 — harmony is consistent and present. Never ship a bare drum-tool sketch or a track without chords. The soul of this music lives in the chord voicings — minor 7ths, minor 9ths, suspended 4ths.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Updating the taste profile

`knowledge/music_context.md` is the file to edit. It is structured in sections with clear headings — the AI Primer block at the top gives agents a fast orientation, while the full sections (genre identity, sonic palette, production DNA, essential cuts, anti-patterns) give depth for production-level decisions.

```bash
# Open the taste profile for editing
open knowledge/music_context.md

# After editing, verify the curation scorer picks it up on next run
python3 music_curation/discovery.py --source labels --days 7
```

The file includes an evolution protocol: update it after any session where something clicked or something missed badly. Tag entries with `[TASTE]`, `[PRODUCTION]`, or `[EXTENSIONS]` for the consolidation script to pick up. Key update triggers:

- New essential cuts identified (add with the specific *why* element)
- Ableton preferences sharpen through production sessions
- A production session reveals something new about the aesthetic
- An AI output lands perfectly — document what worked

<Tip>
  The taste profile is consumed simultaneously by the Ableton Dream Agent (before every nightly sketch), the Reference Finder (when suggesting tracks for harmonic mixing), and the Music Curation engine (as the baseline for all scoring). A single edit to `knowledge/music_context.md` propagates to all three without any additional steps.
</Tip>
